Works in Progress and Under Review

Rumors Across Borders

My book-style dissertation examines how transnational ties shape the information environments, political attitudes, and political behavior of Latinos in the United States. Drawing on original surveys, interviews, and social media data, the project explores how cross-border social networks influence exposure to political information and misinformation.

Dissertation project. Full manuscript not currently available.

Latino Support for Trump

My job market paper investigates how transnational ties and social media exposure contribute to Latino belief in political misinformation and support for Donald Trump. The paper develops and tests a theory of transnational information environments using original survey data.

View manuscript

Mobilizing Anger in Local School Board Politics

Originally developed as a graduate student collaboration with Hilary Izatt, now Assistant Professor at SUNY Binghamton, and Zoe C. Walker, this project examines how rhetoric about discrimination and privilege shapes political mobilization among white Americans.

Language and Misinformation

My co-authored work with Franshelly Martinez-Ortiz explores whether bilingual Latinos perceive messaging reliability differently based on the language of the message.
